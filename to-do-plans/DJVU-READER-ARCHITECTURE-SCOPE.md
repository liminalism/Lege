# DjVu reading for Liminaldoc: architecture scope

**Assessment date:** 24 September 2026. This is a code and published-results review, not a new performance or compatibility test. The `djvu-rs` snapshot reviewed is [`709d468`](https://github.com/matyushkin/djvu-rs/tree/709d46891d1741c9c342470e99e4edf5463a3e87) (`v0.35.0`). The local comparison is [DJVULibRust](lege-codecs/djvulibrust/).

## Recommendation

Use **`djvu-rs` for reading and DJVULibRust for writing**, behind separate optional backends in Liminaldoc. Keep DJVULibRust as the writer on the strength of its focused encoding design and the existing output-quality evidence. Do not build its reader merely because its encoder previously beat `djvu-rs`: the encoder result says little about decoder architecture, and `djvu-rs` now implements most of the reader capabilities that an interactive viewer needs.

This does **not** establish that `djvu-rs` is the faster decoder, nor that its writer has caught up. It says that its *current reading architecture and completed surface* make it the better starting point for the viewer. A future in-house decoder remains reasonable if a concrete limitation survives integration work.

## What the older comparison actually shows

The [local benchmark table](lege-codecs/djvulibrust/README.md#benchmarks) reports a 50-page, same-input IW44/BG44 encode at 100 slices: DJVULibRust page-parallel took **1.918 s**, produced **5,717,668 bytes**, and achieved **37.28 dB** average reconstructed PSNR; the then-tested `djvu-rs` took **5.822 s**, produced **6,946,698 bytes**, and achieved **22.63 dB**. DJVULibRust's serial output matched DjVuLibre's size and PSNR closely in that run. These are meaningful reasons to retain the local **writer**. The table has no decoder timing, and I found the summary and command in the README but not the referenced `iw44-bench-harness` or raw result files in this project, so its date and exact tested `djvu-rs` revision are not recoverable from the checked-in evidence.

The newer `djvu-rs` [encoder parity scorecard](https://github.com/matyushkin/djvu-rs/blob/709d46891d1741c9c342470e99e4edf5463a3e87/BENCHMARKS_RESULTS.md#encoder-parity-scorecard-684-2026-07-13) compares **its encoder with DjVuLibre**, not with DJVULibRust. Its two reported IW44 cases remain larger and lower-PSNR than DjVuLibre; the gap varies widely by page. Different corpus, profile, and machine prevent combining the two tables into a current head-to-head verdict. `djvu-rs` also now has [parallel page preparation and a bounded streaming window](https://github.com/matyushkin/djvu-rs/blob/709d46891d1741c9c342470e99e4edf5463a3e87/src/djvu_encode.rs#L1000-L1050), so page parallelism alone is no longer an exclusive architectural advantage for the local writer.

**Answer to the architecture question:** the local encoder's small, separable page pipeline is still a good architecture and its measured output advantage is worth preserving. It is no longer clearly *architecturally superior overall*: `djvu-rs` now uses similar parallel and bounded-memory ideas while having a much more mature reader architecture. Encoder quality is a reason to keep the local writer, not by itself a reason to build a local reader.

There is an integration seam to plan carefully. Liminaldoc's current [reader engine contract](../liminaldoc/crates/liminal-reader/src/document/engine.rs) assumes a compile stage returns semantic, text, and *compiled display-list* artifacts, then a raster worker consumes them. DjVu pages are decoded layers rather than a PDF display list. Add a DjVu artifact/backend path (or generalize that part of the contract) and map its native page coordinates, rotation, text zones, and annotations to the viewer's geometry. The [tile demand](../liminaldoc/crates/liminal-reader/src/document/tile.rs) and `djvu-rs` display-space tiles are compatible in purpose, but zoom buckets, color modes, pass quality, cancellation tokens, and two cache budgets need explicit translation. This is adapter work, not a reason to write four decoders.

## Decision boundary

Keep the two crates separate and feature-gated so opening a writing document does not initialize a DjVu reader, and opening DjVu does not initialize the writer. For the reader, start with the synchronous backed/mmap and tile APIs; the async range reader is useful later only if remote documents become a requirement. Favor the library's decode-oriented default feature set over its conversion or CLI features. Preserve DjVu-specific behavior within the reader backend rather than moving it into Pixelkit or the common document model.

Reconsider an in-house reader if the adapter exposes a **specific** unmet need that cannot be fixed in `djvu-rs` at reasonable cost: unacceptable first-visible-page latency or decoded-layer memory, missing format coverage, inadequate cancellation, or a persistent geometry/text fidelity problem. Its published [June 2026 render comparison](https://github.com/matyushkin/djvu-rs/blob/709d46891d1741c9c342470e99e4edf5463a3e87/BENCHMARKS_RESULTS.md#L318-L357) shows some 1.2–2.1× gaps against DjVuLibre on its selected pages, so speed is a real question for later measurement, not a proven advantage here. If such a need appears, a targeted improvement or replacement codec may be cheaper than a complete second reader. The quick completion of the JBIG2 decoder is encouraging experience, but DjVu reading also requires container variants, BZZ, ZP, JB2, IW44, shared dictionaries, progressive composition, text/annotations, and resource control.

**Confidence:** High on the present API/architecture difference; moderate on the recommendation until interoperability and viewer behavior can be measured. No new test was run for this assessment.
